## One-line positioning

BairesDev’s Q4 2025 Dev Barometer shows AI redefining the software developer role rather than eliminating it: developers report stronger skills, faster delivery, and a shift toward solution design, AI orchestration, architecture, and strategy.

## Suggested report description

In October 2025, BairesDev surveyed 501 developers and 19 project managers across 92 software development projects in 40 countries. The Q4 2025 Dev Barometer captures how AI changed developer careers in 2025 and what developers expect in 2026. The findings show AI strengthening technical skills, improving work-life balance, accelerating delivery, and pushing developers toward higher-value work such as solution design, AI-generated code integration, architecture, and strategy. The report also includes project-manager views on talent bottlenecks, AI ROI, readiness, risks, maturity, privacy, and high-potential workflows.

## Methodology and respondent profile

- Survey conducted: October 2025
- Developer respondent base: 501 developers
- Project manager respondent base: 19 project managers
- Projects represented: 92 software development projects
- Total professionals surveyed: 520
- Countries represented: 40
- Developer experience: 53% senior-level or higher / 8+ years of experience
- Project context: startups, SMBs, and enterprise projects

Visible distribution:

Deck callout: Average AI maturity level: 2.4 — Emerging Strategy.

Maturity interpretation in deck:

- AI initiatives are moving beyond isolated pilots toward defined goals and partial integration in business operations.
- A formal strategy is taking shape.
- Executive sponsorship is growing.
- Early signs of measurable impact are appearing.
- Adoption remains uneven across teams and use cases.

Maturity definitions:

1. Exploratory: Organization is starting to explore AI with limited research and small-scale experiments, but no defined use cases.
2. Early Pilots: AI pilots are underway in non-critical areas, with recognized potential but no formal strategy or consistent resourcing.
3. Structured Adoption: Multiple use cases and pilots exist with a roadmap for expansion, though integration remains fragmented.
4. Scaled Adoption: AI is embedded across business functions with governance, impact metrics, and cross-functional teams in place.
5. Strategic / Transformational: AI sits at the core of business strategy, fully integrated across areas and driving innovation through proprietary or specialized models.

Content angle: Client AI maturity improved slightly from Q3’s 2.2/5 to Q4’s 2.4/5, but most companies remain far from scaled or transformational AI.

## Executive-ready synthesis

The Q4 2025 report reframes AI’s impact on software engineering. The central finding is not replacement, but role redesign. Developers are reporting stronger skills, better work-life balance, faster delivery, and new career opportunities. But they are also becoming responsible for higher-order work: designing solutions, integrating AI-generated code, evaluating quality, managing risks, and aligning technical decisions with business goals. For technology leaders, the practical implication is to treat AI adoption as an operating-model shift. The teams that benefit most will combine AI tools with stronger QA, clearer requirements, better governance, targeted upskilling, and access to specialized AI/data talent.
